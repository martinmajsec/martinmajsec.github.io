About me
==========

I am a computer science student at the [Faculty of Electrical Engineering and Computing](https://www.fer.unizg.hr/en). 
As part of [MODISTe](https://www.fer.unizg.hr/modiste), 
I completed my [BSc thesis](https://repozitorij.fer.unizg.hr/islandora/object/fer:13985) on an open problem related to spanning trees
under the supervision of prof. dr. sc. Anamari Nakić. 
Outside of studies, I've most recently worked on Virtue, a programming language developed by Zorislav Shoyat that enables automatic parallel program execution.

### Research interests

My core interest lies in algorithmic problem-solving, especially finding useful bounds, and especially via approximation algorithms. Most of what I'm looking into falls into discrete mathematics, predominantly graph theory and combinatorial game theory.

Furthermore, one of my favorite mental exercises is programming language and compiler design. While I'm looking for domain-specific problems that could use a new language, I am putting it into practice via static code analysis for Apertus, a [SwissAI](https://www.swiss-ai.org/) project.

I'm especially interested in leveraging domain-specific settings to get better performance. In particular, I like domain-specific computer architecture and languages, but also playing with restricted theoretical models of e.g. computation or memory management.

Finally, I'm excited about formalizing notions that haven't been thoroughly explored in mathematics. Good examples are strategy-proof mechanisms for the [Strategic Facility Location Problem](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3127550/1/Mechanism_Design_for_Facility_Location_Problem__A_Survey%20(13).pdf) and problems studied by [Erik Demaine](https://erikdemaine.org/) such as the [Conveyer-Belt Problem](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/ConveyerAlphabet_Elasticity/).

### Competitive programming

I teach competitive programming to elementary and high school students at Zagrebački Računalni Savez.
Over the course of four years, I have regularly mentored over 20 students, in groups of 4–8 students per year.
The lectures are primarily designed to prepare students for the state competitions Infokup and [COCI](https://hsin.hr/coci/announcement.html), where participants face problems that range from ad hoc implementation challenges to clever use of well-known algorithms.
